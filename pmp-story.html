People Leadership

Building Excellence
Through People

How investing in a team's growth created a culture of professional excellence
Zahirudeen Premji  •  IBM/Mylex → LSI Corporation  •  2002 – 2005
The Situation

A Team Without a Common Standard

When I became Director of Program Management at IBM/Mylex in 2002, I inherited 26 direct reports across program management and technical publications, managing 40 concurrent projects under a $3.5 million budget.
The team was capable but inconsistent. Each program manager had their own methodology, their own templates, their own definition of "done." There was no shared professional standard — and no shared professional identity.

The Approach

Investing in People,
Not Just Process

I didn't mandate certification. I invested in it. I arranged study groups, paired experienced practitioners with newer team members, and created time in the schedule for preparation.
This wasn't a checkbox exercise — it was about building a shared language and a shared standard of excellence. I led through conversation, encouragement, and accountability — not through pressure.
The Journey

One by One,
the Team Transformed

Over 18 months, program managers began passing the PMP exam. Each certification was celebrated. Each success built momentum for the next person. The culture shifted from "that's how I do it" to "that's how we do it."

Near-100% Certification
Achieved

Within 18 months, virtually every program manager earned their PMP. The initiative continued seamlessly through the IBM/Mylex to LSI transition — proof that the culture of excellence had taken root in the team itself.

The Leadership Principle

I build innovation
through people.

When you invest in your team's professional growth — not as a mandate but as a shared aspiration — you create something more durable than any process improvement. You create a culture that sustains itself.
