In 2007, LSI acquired StoreAge — an Israeli storage software company — to strengthen its high-availability enterprise storage portfolio. I was appointed Integration Manager for the Engenio division, reporting directly to the CEO. My mandate: bring two companies, two cultures, and two engineering organizations into one.

The Challenge
TWO
COMPANIES
ONE VISION
StoreAge's product was a complementary fit — high-availability storage software designed to work alongside LSI's direct-access enterprise systems. But complementary on paper and integrated in practice are very different problems. Engineering teams on opposite sides of the world, different development cultures, different definitions of done.

My Role

Cross-organizational integration across every LSI department — and hands-on time in Israel working side-by-side with StoreAge's engineering team. Both companies on the phone, both cultures in the room, one integration to deliver.

The Honest Account
WHEN THE
PRODUCT
WON'T SHIP
The new product presented a problem no integration plan could solve: it would not meet its resilience and high-availability targets. Repeated development and test cycles failed to produce a shippable result.

This was not a failure of leadership or process. It was a fundamental engineering challenge that proved more difficult than anyone — on either side of the acquisition — had anticipated. The integration was delivered. The organizational mandate was met. The product problem was inherited.

When new engineering leadership arrived in 2010, eight directors were restructured out — a standard reset for incoming executives. The work done in 2007 and 2008 remained the foundation everything else was built on.
